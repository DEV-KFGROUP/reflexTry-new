Two classic lab tasks show up in Reflextry’s reflex arena for good reason: **Go/No-Go** stresses stopping a prepared action, and **Flanker** stresses selecting a target while ignoring conflicting neighbors. Both punish reckless speed.

## Go/No-Go in one paragraph

Most trials say Go (press). A minority say No-Go (do not press). The high Go rate builds a prepotent press habit—so the rare stop trial becomes the real test. Score good Go hits **and** successful withholds. False alarms (pressing on No-Go) reveal impulsivity under time pressure.

### Training cues

- Keep the finger ready, not tense.  
- After a long Go streak, expect a No-Go.  
- Slightly slower clean Go hits beat hyperfast hits with constant false alarms.

## Flanker in one paragraph

Five arrows appear. Respond only to the **center** direction. Congruent trials agree; incongruent trials put flankers in opposition. Conflict slows you and invites errors because two response plans activate and one must be suppressed.

### Training cues

- Fixate the center character before the string “votes” as a crowd.  
- Do not majority-vote with the flankers.  
- Raise incongruent accuracy before hunting average RT.

## How they differ

| Task | Core demand | Typical failure |
|------|-------------|-----------------|
| Go/No-Go | Inhibit a prepared press | False alarms after Go streaks |
| Flanker | Select amid spatial conflict | Following flanker direction |

If you false-alarm constantly, start with Go/No-Go. If you are accurate on stops but collapse when symbols conflict, emphasize Flanker.

## Choice reaction sits nearby

Choice RT adds multiple illuminated targets without a withhold rule. It is decision latency among alternatives. Many players need all three: simple RT, choice RT, and inhibition/conflict drills.

## Session design

1. Two minutes easy warm-up (simple RT or slow Go).  
2. One focused Go/No-Go block emphasizing zero false alarms.  
3. One Flanker block emphasizing incongruent accuracy.  
4. Stop at 12–15 minutes while quality is high.

## What improvement looks like

- Fewer false alarms at the same Go RT  
- Higher incongruent Flanker accuracy  
- Tighter RT variance on correct trials  

Not a cinematic “brain unlocked” montage.

## Honesty about transfer

These paradigms are used in cognitive neuroscience for a reason—they isolate executive control components. Browser practice can sharpen those components in the task. Real-world impulse control still depends on sleep, stress, and context. Use the tools as precise drills, then apply the same “pause before acting” habit where it matters.

## Warm-up that protects inhibition

Cold starts produce false alarms. Spend one minute on slow Go-only responses (if your mode allows practice) or simple reaction, then begin scored Go/No-Go. For Flanker, do ten congruent-only mental rehearsals: “center only” as a verbal cue before the first incongruent block.

## Interpreting mixed results

Fast Go RT + many false alarms → impulsivity dominant.  
Slow Go RT + clean withholds → overly cautious; carefully raise readiness without mashing.  
Flanker congruent fine / incongruent poor → conflict control is the bottleneck, not vision.

Write one sentence diagnosis after each session. That habit beats screenshots of vanity scores.

## Coaching kids or teammates

Keep instructions short: “Green means go. Red means freeze.” For Flanker: “Only the middle arrow.” Celebrate clean withholds as loudly as fast hits—otherwise learners optimize the wrong number.
